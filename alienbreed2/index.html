Alien Breed II · rebuild

The page contains no game data. The game is unlocked by your own Alien Breed II disk images (ADF, disks 1 and 2); they stay in your browser.

E switches. Original: the game's own 320-pixel window, exactly as the Amiga drew it. Enhanced: a wider view (enemies come from beyond the old edge), smooth motion and a softer CRT. Every setting is kept per mode.

Size fit

Pixel width works like the width knob of a monitor: 1.00 = square pixels, 1.04 = PAL. Smooth motion draws the play area at your display's refresh rate; the game itself still runs at 50 Hz.

A gamepad or USB joystick shows up after you press one of its buttons.

Controls

Arrows or WASD = player 1, Ctrl / Z = fire. Player 2: I J K L, right Shift / Enter = fire, or a second gamepad. M map, Space Intex terminal. Gamepad: stick / d-pad, A / B / X / right trigger = fire. Touch controls appear on phones and tablets.

E: Original / Enhanced. F: full screen. P: pause. R: restart. Esc: close the menu.

The original plays 4 channels with fixed roles and drops some sounds when they collide (about 7 %). Enhanced sound moves them to a free channel. The reactor hum gets louder as you approach a working reactor, comes from its side in stereo and fades when it is destroyed. Kept per mode.

Sound starts after a click or a key press (a browser rule).

    About this rebuild

    Thanks for playing! This preview ends here – the full game will follow.

    Alien Breed II: The Horror Continues (Team17, 1993) rebuilt for the browser from the game's own code: the 68000 code on your disks is statically translated to C and WebAssembly with the AREK kit, and the Amiga chipset (bus slots, Copper, Blitter, bitplanes, sprites, Paula) is a model checked against an emulator.

    Measured against the original in an emulator (level 1, four 30-second runs with random joystick input): 99.9 % of the pixels match, the sound chip writes match in 97–100 % of the frames, and the game state matches tick by tick for the whole run in one of the four – in the others small CPU timing differences make the runs drift apart after 1–3 seconds. Enhanced draws a wider view from the level map and the game's own drawing, and moves smoothly at your display's rate.

    After Start the game boots as it did from floppies: its intro, title, hall of fame and credits take about 1½ minutes and end in a demo that plays itself. Fire (Ctrl / Z or a gamepad button) skips the intro; on the title screen fire starts the game, and level 1 is reached in about 25 seconds.

    On the way we found two bugs in the original game: explosion debris can fly through a wall and off the map (the game then reads memory outside the map – harmless on an Amiga), and the sound driver can mix up a sound number (a leftover high byte), which silences a channel. Both are handled without changing how the original plays.
